# RL concepts for the first experiment

At each control step, the environment gives the agent an **observation**, the agent chooses an **action**, and the environment advances and returns a **reward** plus a possible end signal. This interaction defines the part of a Markov decision process we can program. The underlying **state** is all information needed to predict the next state; an observation may omit some of it. CartPole supplies cart position, cart speed, pole angle, and pole angular speed, so the policy sees motion as well as pose. On the robot, the IMU supplies pitch and pitch rate, but no encoder means wheel position and speed are missing.

A **policy** maps observations to actions. In CartPole the action is a discrete push left or right. For balancing it will be a continuous signed motor command, possibly mapped to both wheels. The policy is a neural network whose weights are adjusted during training. A **reward** tells the learner what outcomes are preferred; CartPole's +1 per surviving step rewards long episodes. An **episode** ends on a fall, cart position limit, or time limit. A **return** is the sum of rewards over an episode, often discounted during learning with factor `gamma` so nearer rewards weigh a little more.

PPO repeatedly collects trajectories using its current policy, estimates which actions did better than expected, then changes the policy by limited steps. Its **critic** estimates future return; the **advantage** is roughly observed return minus that estimate. PPO's clipping discourages a single update from making the new policy too different from the policy that collected the data. This stabilizes training but does not guarantee convergence or transfer to hardware. `n_steps` controls how much experience is collected before an update; `batch_size` and `n_epochs` control how often that experience is reused. These are training settings, not microcontroller settings. [SB3's PPO guide](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html) and the [PPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347) give the algorithm details.

During **training**, the learner explores and updates actor and critic weights. During **inference**, the saved actor receives one observation and computes an action without changing weights. The deployment goal is deterministic actor inference; the critic and PPO optimizer stay on the laptop.

For the robot, a useful reward must match the physical objective and actuator limits. An agent can exploit a badly chosen reward: for example, if reward only penalizes pitch error, it may accelerate indefinitely while staying momentarily upright. Position and wheel speed penalties address this only if the observation and measurements support the task. Normalize physical quantities using fixed scales shared by Python and firmware, and test reward variants against drift, effort and disturbance recovery rather than training return alone.
