## First simulation-only PPO software milestone — 2026-10-14

`environments/wall_e_balance_env.py` now wraps `simulation/models/wall_e_balance.xml` in the Gymnasium reset/step API. It uses the separate image-derived N20 assumption config, one signed continuous action, fused simulated MPU pitch/rate and two **simulator-only** wheel speeds, and a configurable upright/effort reward. The environment bypasses the PID controller. It resets from `qpos0` with a small axle-centered pitch perturbation, terminates at the configured fall tilt, and truncates at the episode time limit. The real TT motors have no encoders, so this observation vector cannot be exported as a deployable policy.

`training/train_wall_e_ppo.py` and `training/evaluate_wall_e_ppo.py` are separate from the CartPole workflow. The environment tests and SB3 `check_env` passed; a 256-step headless smoke run produced a checkpoint, final policy, TensorBoard log, config/versions, validation data, and independent evaluation. The 10-episode final evaluation had **100% falls** and mean survival **0.187 s**. A separate 30-episode fresh-seed/tilt evaluation also had **100% falls** and mean survival **0.188 s**. This demonstrates a reproducible software path, **not learned balance or Phase 3 physics validation**. Next, measure the actual motor/driver response and wheel sensing strategy, check contact/inertia against the assembled robot, then redesign observations and training before interpreting reward improvement.

The 2026-10-14 repository cleanup placed optional PID/IMU diagnostics under `simulation/diagnostics/`, inspection renders under `simulation/renders/`, PDF regeneration scripts under `docs/builders/`, and older CAD exports under `archive/cad/`. The active `Assembly 1/` source, generated MJCFs, experiment records, training paths, and firmware were preserved. Current commands are in `simulation/README.md`.

## Decisions before Phase 3

- The active static geometry-only MuJoCo preview uses `Assembly 1/assembly_1/urdf/assembly_1.urdf`. It preserves three rigid visual groups and records wheel hinge frames, but contains no physical inertias, dynamic joints, contacts, or actuators. This is not Phase 3 physics validation; the measurements below remain required.
- The mass/COM audit now assigns `part_1`=32 g and `part_1_1`=`part_2`=4.42 g each (40.84 g combined). The 30 g PCB scenario estimates 379.839766 g total; the reconstructed-PCB scenario estimates 379.767823 g, closely matching the 379.768 g Onshape screenshot. The static preview and COM-inspection XMLs carry mass/COM as audit metadata only; the separate dynamic/balance XMLs have provisional physical inertials and joints. Compare the audit with hardware measurements and validate aggregate inertias before Phase 3; see `simulation/MASS_AND_COM_REPORT.md`.

- A user-supplied N20-12V-200 specification image was used for a separate motor-model experiment (`configs/mujoco_balance_n20_assumption.yaml`). A 52,521-point PID grid at 0° found no candidate surviving 2 s; the top-ranked candidate fell at 0.35 s. This is not a measured motor response or a Phase 2 exit: motor identity/direction, loaded response, battery behavior, contacts and inertia still require characterization. See `docs/MUJOCO_N20_ASSUMED_PID_REPORT.pdf`.

- The user confirms the assembled robot uses the local `/home/varun/Wall-E-2027-Board-Design/sra_board.kicad_sch` design and an MPU6050 IMU. The schematic has an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 symbol and TB6612FNG motor-driver module. Confirm the driver marking, IMU mounting and interface timing during characterization. The older linked [SRA 2026 repository](https://github.com/SRA-VJTI/sra-board-hardware-design) uses an ESP32-WROOM-32E.
- A MuJoCo sensor/actuator scaffold now exists (`simulation/build_balance_model.py`, `imu_model.py`, `balance_controller.py`, `motor_model.py`, `run_balance.py`). The user supplied the IMU center and axis directions, which are configured in the separate balance MJCF; the dynamic model also now begins with tire-floor contact and its verifier checks the initial contacts. This is still not Phase 3 physics completion: masses/inertias, wheel actuation signs, motor response, calibrated contact/friction, chassis collision geometry, and balance behavior need validation. Synthetic plumbing checks and the tiny initial contact preload are not hardware validation. See `simulation/README.md` and `simulation/DYNAMIC_MODEL_REPORT.md`.
- On 2026-10-11, ran a reset-to-identical-state diagnostic PID sweep at setpoints 23–30° using a fixed 0.02 N·m ideal torque scale and signs `(-1,+1)`. All eight trials failed the stability criteria, approached ±90° pitch, and had gyro saturation in 154–160 of 200 samples. CSV telemetry, summary, and a 168-frame animated preview are in `experiments/mujoco_setpoint_sweep/20261011T080208Z/`. These are artificial-actuator simulation results, not evidence about the real motor or the real robot. The sweep is useful mainly as a failure diagnosis: fix the IMU startup/offset behavior, verify actuator polarity, and improve plant/contact realism before interpreting setpoint effects.
- On 2026-10-12, swept every integer IMU pitch setpoint from −90° through +90° for 5 s each (181 trials) from the same initial state, retaining the firmware-compatible gains and explicit test-only 0.02 N·m torque scale with provisional signs `(-1,+1)`. **Zero candidates passed** the screen (mean absolute last-window error <2°, pitch span <3°, no gyro saturation, and contact). Every trial saturated gyro repeatedly and reached near-fall pitch; the closest signed mean error was at −15° (−0.18°), but its last-window pitch span was 57.2° and peak absolute pitch was 89.7°, so it was not balancing. The summary, full telemetry, and exact run metadata are at `experiments/mujoco_setpoint_sweep/20261012T134526Z/`. This rejects setpoint-only search under this provisional plant/controller setup; it does not prove the physical robot cannot balance. Do not transfer any candidate to hardware. Next resolve sensor angle-zero/sign, verify motor polarity/authority, and characterize actuator/contact behavior before retuning the controller or model.
- On 2026-10-13, corrected the MuJoCo balance controller to match the firmware's PID-correction magnitude and error-sign motor direction, enabled the MPU6050's integer gyro-count division by default, added CLI overrides for setpoint/Kp/Ki/Kd, and added automatic episode reset at configurable total chassis tilt. The optional `--tune` mode opens live setpoint/gain/fall-angle sliders in a companion window, with pause/resume and manual reset; the MuJoCo viewer stays open across episode resets. A 3 s headless plumbing run at 25° fall threshold logged 300 samples and 16 resets; a 2 s GUI smoke run logged 200 samples and 11 resets. Both used artificial torque and provisional signs, so they verify software/UI/reset plumbing only—not balancing or physical motor behavior. Details and commands are in `simulation/README.md`.
- On 2026-10-13, inspected the repeated falling and found two software/model causes: `qpos0` starts at the CAD chassis pose while reset-time MuJoCo acceleration makes the first raw accelerometer pitch read about 39.7° at near-zero geometric pitch; and firmware-style PWM enforces a 60% minimum outside the deadband, so low/zero PID gains cannot command gentle effort. The controller now seeds the complementary filter from gravity projected through the reset-pose IMU frame, then resumes using full sensor readings. Live `--tune` defaults to an explicitly simulation-only continuous-magnitude PWM mode (0–80%) so gains affect motor effort; normal runs remain firmware-clamped unless `--pwm-output-mode continuous` is selected. Telemetry records the seed pitch and run metadata records reset-pose and PWM modes. This fixes startup-estimator inconsistency and makes gain effects inspectable; it does not repair the CAD-derived COM offset, characterize motors, or demonstrate balance. Verification: focused controller/IMU tests and `simulation.verify_balance_io`; a short integration capture is under `experiments/mujoco_balance/20261013T_continuous_mode_check/`.
- A 700-candidate randomized search was run with continuous effort, 0.02 N·m artificial torque and provisional signs `(left=+1, right=-1)`. A candidate (setpoint 30.637°, Kp 3.7754, Ki 0.00897, Kd 11.2316) survived the 12 s validation, but its 60 s follow-up accelerated the wheels to ~491 rad/s and held the chassis near 28° pitch. This is a leaned, runaway-wheel trajectory, not balance. A separate 700-candidate upright-zero search did not exceed 0.63 s. The experiment and scoring changes are in `simulation/diagnostics/tune_balance_pid.py` and `experiments/mujoco_pid_search/20261013T_refined/`, `.../20261013T_upright_zero_retry/`; long-run telemetry is in `experiments/mujoco_balance/20261013T_long_validation/`. Do not adopt those gains: ideal constant torque lacks measured motor back-EMF/speed limits, and the angle-zero/balance reference is uncalibrated. Next: characterize direction and motor response, measure actual balance-pose MPU pitch, then repeat tuning with a validated plant.
- The MuJoCo config now sets `angle_zero_deg: 0.0` and `pitch_setpoint_deg: 0.0`, defining its `qpos0` reset pose as the simulated upright reference. `verify_balance_io` checks the reset gravity-seeded pitch matches this target. This is a simulation reference only, not a measured hardware balance angle or a balance result.
- The first three stationary captures showed fused outputs starting near zero and converging toward roll −20.25° / pitch +6.19°. The later 2026-10-12 repeat set settled instead near −13.70° / −0.31°, so the physical orientation was likely not reproduced between sessions. `simulation/imu_model.py` now replays the latest set using +13.86° roll and −1.50° pitch seed offsets with 9.2 s decay; this stays disabled by default because neither pose is a confirmed balancing reference. It does not identify raw sensor bias or the drift mechanism. Next measure the pose mechanically and log raw accelerometer/gyro alongside fused angles in the actual balancing firmware. See `docs/HARDWARE.md` and `simulation/README.md`.
- A second three-run hardware capture set on 2026-10-12 is saved at `experiments/hardware_imu/repeat_20261012T132500Z/` (raw logs, summary CSV, metadata). The runs are fairly repeatable with one another, but settle near roll −13.70° / pitch −0.31°, not the previous −20.25° / +6.19°. This points to a different physical orientation between sessions, or failure to reproduce the earlier pose; the pose was not independently measured. The sim replay now reflects this latest set, remains disabled by default, and is pose-specific. Capture raw accel/gyro counts and mechanically document the orientation next.
- The current TT motors have no encoders. Wheel position and wheel speed in the proposed six-element observation are unavailable. An IMU can estimate pitch and pitch rate, but integrating acceleration to recover wheel motion will drift. An encoderless policy can try to keep pitch upright yet gradually drive away; stationary balance needs a position reference, reliable visual/optical motion sensing, or added encoders. Select the actual observation vector only after deciding this.
- The provisional reward's position and velocity terms cannot be evaluated on hardware without these measurements. Training with perfect simulator-only wheel state would create an observation mismatch. Reward can use simulator states during training, but such a reward does not solve missing deployable observations. Keep train and deploy observations identical.
- Determine torque versus PWM and battery voltage empirically. L-shaped TT motors vary significantly by gear ratio and supply; no nominal specification is assumed here.
- MuJoCo is the user's preferred simulator for Phase 3. Keep a simpler analytic model as a validation reference if useful.
- Start with PPO and one equal command for both motors. Preserve independent safety checks outside the policy. Treat 100 Hz and a 6–32–32–1 network as targets to measure and revise.

## First robot measurements

1. Weigh the assembled robot and each wheel/motor subassembly. Measure wheel loaded radius, axle distance, and center-of-mass height with the battery installed; record measurement uncertainty and battery placement. Compare the measurements with `simulation/models/wall_e_mass_audit.json` and investigate any discrepancy rather than treating the export-based estimate as a measurement.
2. Confirm IMU identity, mounting orientation, interface and sample timing. Log raw accelerometer and gyro while still and during supported motion to estimate noise, bias and latency.
3. On a stand, sweep safe PWM duty in both directions at several battery voltages. Log command, voltage and observable wheel response to identify polarity, dead zone, asymmetry, saturation and delay. With no encoders, use temporary optical/tachometer video measurement or consider adding encoders.
4. Confirm motor supply range, driver wiring and current limits. Compare actual controller revision against the reference schematic before assigning pins or firmware peripherals.


## Stationary simulation PPO result — 2026-10-14

The separate `configs/wall_e_stationary_ppo.yaml` adds simulator-only axle position
and velocity to the original IMU/wheel observations (six values), with bounded
position/speed reward and 40% PWM cap. It uses the CAD COM-over-axle reset angle
32.5 +/- 3 degrees. Per-physics-step motor torque updates are retained; stationary
observations synchronize pose/sensor fields after integration. A zero-speed motor
friction bug was corrected: sub-breakaway electrical torque no longer produces
reverse net motor torque. Manufacturer N20 endpoints and physical model XML are
unchanged; motor interpolation/contact and encoderless sensing remain unvalidated.

Selected run: `experiments/wall_e_ppo/seed42042_20261013T215415483354Z`; use `best/best_model.zip` at 114,688 steps, selected by
validation reward. Its final checkpoint at 200,704 steps degraded (up to 32.5 cm
excursion in 10 s), so do not substitute `final_model.zip`. Fresh deterministic
tests passed 20/20 at 30 s and a further 20/20 at 60 s: no falls, maximum axle
excursion 4.32 cm/4.30 cm, worst settled RMS speed 1.884/1.793 cm/s respectively.
Success thresholds remain 5 cm excursion, 2 cm/s settled RMS speed, 2 degrees
settled RMS pitch error after 1 s, and full-duration survival. These are chosen
simulation tolerances, not hardware specifications. One-seed 30 s checks at 2,
1 and 0.5 ms physics steps all survived with no warnings and <=4.16 cm excursion.
27 tests plus 6 subtests, SB3 checker/random-action checks and balance IO/dynamic
verification passed. Source snapshots, logs and independent results are saved;
`stationary_result.json` summarizes the experiment and limitations. Commands are
in `simulation/README.md`. Small corrective oscillation remains, and no disturbance
or sim-to-real robustness is established. CartPole, firmware, CAD and XMLs were
not modified in this stationary experiment.
